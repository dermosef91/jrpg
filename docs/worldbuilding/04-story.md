# Story Arc

> **Full spoilers.** This document states the buried premise first so that the act
> beats are legible. For the player-facing sequence of discovery, read from
> "Act I" down.

## The buried thing

The Old Boles survived the last Long Dusk by discovering that Striders can be induced
to **root-fuse** — dozens of separate organisms grafted into a single supercolony that
pools light and survives the dark.

It works. Everyone alive is descended from it.

Two costs were suppressed:

1. **A fused canopy supports roughly a third of the combined population.** They did not
   merely merge — they **selected**. The criteria remain recoverable from ring chemistry.
2. **Fusion blends the ring-record.** Forty-one distinct peoples, migration histories,
   and cant-lineages were overwritten into one continuous fabricated history. Modern
   Bole society's founding truth — *"we have always been one people"* — is an artifact
   of the merge. They did not simply lose their cultures. They lost the capacity to
   know they had lost anything.

As the Ember recovered, the fused supercolony dispersed again, budding off eleven
daughter Boles over eleven generations. Every Bole alive is a fragment of one merge.

**The Remain** are the descendants of those who refused selection, walked down, and
survived the entire Long Dusk on the diffuse light of the forest floor. Poorer,
smaller, and completely intact. They kept everything, orally, because they had no rings
to be edited.

**Who edited the record:** the **cant-singers**. Their lineages are the only ones who
can write to ring-memory. The original edit was performed as an act of mercy toward
traumatized survivors. It has been maintained for eleven generations since as inherited
duty — each singer taught to re-ink certain passages without being told why, only that
the passage must hold. A ritual whose meaning was lost, performed faithfully, by people
who love their grandmothers.

## ACT I — The Audit

*Scope: one dispute. Tone: procedural, low-stakes, deeply lived-in.*

The party is a licensed audit circuit, not a band of adventurers. Establish the world
through mundane work.

**Beat 1 — The job.** **Cordwain** (home Bole, mid-sized, deep in sunwood debt) is
contesting a migration-rights claim against **Anneal** (largest Bole, Pruner-dominated
council). The party is dispatched to read the disputed rings. Cordwain's rings support
Cordwain's claim. Clean, done, go home — except the Coring notices the physical grain
does not match the chemical record in a section nobody asked about.

**Beat 2 — The discrepancy.** A ~40-ring stretch is chemically *too consistent*. Real
memory is noisy; this is smooth. Someone wrote it. The Cant-singer recognizes the
technique as her own lineage's hand, and cannot say when it was done or by whom. Her
confusion is genuine.

**Beat 3 — Widening.** Comparison cores from two neighboring Boles, pulled as due
diligence, carry the same 40-ring stretch, the same smoothing, the same hand.

**Beat 4 — The wrong question.** The party assumes forgery-for-profit and spends a
substantial stretch of the act building a solid, plausible, *wrong* case: that Anneal
falsified migration records to seize sunwood rights. **Verity Anneal-of-the-Ninth**
dismantles the case publicly, without malice, and then privately tells them she has
seen the same smoothing in Anneal's own rings and has sat on it for six years because
she does not want to know.

**Act I turn.** It is not a forgery — it is an **edit**, present in every Bole, at the
same depth, in the same hand. Which means it predates the separation of the Boles.
Someone rewrote the founding of the world. The only records that cannot have been
touched are the **Old Boles** in the east. The party files for a survey writ, Anneal's
council denies it, and they go anyway — becoming criminals in the most tedious possible
way: expired paperwork.

## ACT II — Hollow Sen

*Scope: the continent. Tone: the floor drops out twice.*

**Beat 5 — The dead Bole.** **Hollow Sen** is a standing corpse of a Strider: canopy
collapsed millennia ago, root-crown intact and readable. The act's tentpole location is
a dungeon that is a library made of wood, where progress is *reading* and the hazards
are structural rather than hostile.

**Midpoint reveal — fusion.** Sen's rings show root-fusion scarring where a dozen
Striders were grafted into one. It worked. The party's first reaction is **elation** —
this is a survival technique, this is the answer, they can take it home. The middle of
Act II is genuinely hopeful. Let them organize it. Let them win political fights. Let
Marrow's Splicers get excited.

**Beat 6 — The Remain.** Forced to ground between Boles, the party shelters with
Groundwalkers through a storm — an inconvenience, not an encounter. The Remain listen
to the excited account of fusion and go quiet. Then an elder recites, from memory, the
names of **forty-one** Boles that entered the fusion and the **eleven** that came out.
The party has never heard forty of those names.

**Beat 7 — Selection.** Back at Sen, now knowing what to look for, the Cant-singer and
Graft-medic reconstruct the criteria from ring chemistry. This is the coldest scene in
the game and it must be *quiet*: an afternoon of careful lab work that ends with a list.
Who was kept. Metabolic efficiency, reproductive age, craft utility — and, weighted
heavily, **cant-lineage**. The singers selected for singers. Whether that was
self-preservation or infrastructural necessity is genuinely unresolvable, and the party
will argue about it for the rest of the game.

**Beat 8 — The Cant-singer's turn.** She realizes that the passages she was taught to
re-ink as a girl, in her grandmother's kitchen, as an act of love, are the eleventh
generation of the cover-up. **Do not resolve this.** She does not renounce her lineage
and does not turn. She keeps performing the maintenance, because the record physically
degrades without it and letting it rot destroys the evidence too. She becomes
simultaneously the member most insistent on disclosure and the one who must keep lying
to preserve what would be disclosed.

**Act II low point.** Word leaks — partially, and in the worst possible framing.
Marrow's Splicers hear *"fusion works"* and nothing else, and initiate an unsanctioned
graft between **Marrow** and **Sill**, the Stillwood Bole that had chosen to die in
place. It fails catastrophically. Both Striders begin necrotizing; thousands are
displaced to the ground. Verity, vindicated, moves to Prune **Cordwain** — the party's
home — to bank resources against what is coming. On the numbers, she is right.

## ACT III — The Long Dusk

*Scope: everything. Tone: no clean answer, and the game knows it.*

**Beat 9 — Early.** The Ember dims ahead of every projection. Nobody has the years they
planned for. Every slow political process collapses into a single migration season.

**Beat 10 — Three viable paths, no correct one.** The party's job is not to fight but
to **convene** — to get Anneal, Marrow's survivors, the Stillwood remnant, the
Undercanopy Compact, and the Remain into one room, having spent two acts earning the
standing to do it.

- **Fuse.** It works. It is the only option with historical proof. It costs two-thirds
  of the population by selection, and it will blend the record a second time —
  overwriting the truth the party spent the entire game excavating, along with every
  Bole's separate identity. Verity argues for this. She is not wrong.
- **Descend.** Follow the Remain. Abandon the Striders to die; give up the sunwood
  economy, the Ring Councils, and the entire vertical civilization. Nobody is selected.
  Nobody dies who does not have to. Everyone loses the world they know. The Remain will
  help, and will not pretend to be gracious about it.
- **Bridge.** The untested third way: seasonal root-bridges instead of permanent fusion
  — temporary resource-sharing grafts, dissolved before the records blend. Requires
  Splicer technique, Stillwood patience, Remain floor-knowledge, and a Bole willing to
  serve as bridge node, which will kill that Strider. Cordwain volunteers. It may not
  work. There is no precedent, because the last generation was too frightened to try —
  and may have been right.

**Beat 11 — The real climax is the disclosure.** Before any vote, the party decides
whether to make the selection criteria public. Publishing detonates the legitimacy of
every Ring Council mid-crisis and may make coordinated action impossible. Suppressing
makes them the twelfth generation. This is the choice the entire game has been about,
and it must be resolvable either way with the game refusing to grade it.

**Beat 12 — Aftermath, not victory.** Whatever is chosen, close on ring-reading: a short
epilogue in which you read the rings of the season you have just lived through, written
in the party's own hand, and choose what to record about it — including whether to
record it honestly. Final shot: someone eleven generations from now pulling that core.

## Verity Anneal-of-the-Ninth

Recurring pressure, not a villain. She appears in all three acts, is never fought, is
never wrong about arithmetic, and never once lies to the party.

If the Bridge path is taken, she is the person who makes it logistically possible —
while stating on the record that she believes it will kill more people than fusion
would have.

She should be the character players argue about.
