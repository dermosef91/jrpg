# The Map

There is no continent name. Nobody has seen the whole landmass and nobody has a reason
to. You do not map territory in this world — you map **routes**. Cartography is the
practice of naming and dating **Traces**.

## The map primitive: Traces

A Strider's root-crown drags. Over centuries it carves a furrow — kilometer-wide,
tens of meters deep, of tilled and shattered ground — trailing behind every Bole for
its entire migration history.

Traces are roads, ruins, and archives at once:

- **Roads.** The ground is cleared, water pools in them, and they run exactly where
  cities have historically wanted to go.
- **Ruins.** Everything a Bole ever dropped, buried, or built at ground level lies
  strewn along its Trace in stratigraphic order. Dungeon-crawling here is *downstream
  archaeology*.
- **Archives.** Trace depth and weathering date precisely. An auditor reads a Trace the
  way they read rings — and unlike a ring-record, **a Trace cannot be edited.** It is a
  hundred kilometers of physical fact.

This is the map-level rhyme with the game's theme: the party cannot trust the written
record, so they navigate by scars.

## Layout

The axis runs **west (now) → east (origin)**. Everyone lives west. Everyone must go
east. Going east means returning to where the last Long Dusk's selection happened.

```
   W  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────►  E
        dispersed · dimming              ascending · origin

  ┌──────────────┐  ┌─────────┐  ┌──────────┐   ┌──────────────┐
  │  SLACKWATER  │──│   THE   │──│   THE    │───│ THE SENREACH │
  │    TRACES    │  │  BRAID  │  │ GLASSING │   │  (Old Boles) │
  │  ◆ Sill      │  │ ◆Cordwain│ │  (empty) │   │  ◆ Hollow Sen│
  └──────┬───────┘  │ ◆Anneal  │ └────┬─────┘   └──────────────┘
         │          └────┬─────┘      │
    ┌────┴─────┐    ┌────┴────┐   ┌───┴──────┐
    │THE UNDER-│    │ THE SUMP│   │ THE LONG │
    │   LOAM   │    │ ◆Marrow │   │  SHADE   │
    │(the Remain)   └─────────┘   │(barrier) │
    └──────────┘                  └──────────┘
```

**Why east is survival:** the Ember's angle is dropping, and low-angle light only clears
high ground. The Glassing is the last elevation that still catches it.

**Why east is unbearable:** the fusion happened out there. The migration is a return to
the scene.

## Regions

### 1. The Slackwater Traces — *Act I, starting region*

The oldest and deepest Traces, long since waterlogged into a network of canals. Travel
is by flat-bottomed boat *inside* the grooves your ancestors' cities carved. Ancient,
over-farmed, dimming first.

Holds **Sill**, the Stillwood Faith Bole, which has formally resolved to stop migrating
and die in place. Sill is a town with excellent services and a public countdown —
everyone knows the year the light fails, and they have decorated for it. The tutorial
region is a beautiful, well-run community that has scheduled its own end.

### 2. The Braid — *Acts I–III, hub*

Three river-courses cross the light-corridor here; the only place with sufficient water
*and* light for a dense cluster. Consequently the political center, the most contested
ground, and the most scarred terrain in the world — a chaos of overlapping furrows from
eleven generations of jockeying.

Holds **Cordwain** (home Bole — mid-sized, sunwood-indebted, three council seats it
cannot afford) and **Anneal** (largest, richest, Pruner-dominated, Verity's seat).
Anneal is built into a Strider with a split trunk from old damage, so the city is two
crowns that despise each other and share a root system — the world's politics rendered
as one building.

### 3. The Sump — *Act II*

Low, wet, poorly lit, agriculturally worthless — which is exactly why the Splicers were
allowed to work there unsupervised. **Marrow** sits in it: a Strider so heavily grafted
it no longer has coherent species identity. Mismatched bark, canopy sections that leaf
on different schedules, a root-crown surgically forked for speed. Marrow is genuinely
faster than any other Bole and genuinely sick. The body-horror region, and the best
source of the technique the endgame requires.

### 4. The Underloam — *Act II, recontextualization*

Ordinary old-growth forest, unrelated to Striders, with a real canopy and a dim, stable
floor. Home of **the Remain**.

Structurally this must play as a **town region, not a dungeon** — settled, competent,
unbothered. The design goal is that the party arrives braced for wilderness and finds
infrastructure.

### 5. The Long Shade — *Act II gate*

A range running southwest–northeast whose shadow is thrown far north by the low Ember.
Not a mountain to climb — a **shadow to time**. The shade-line sweeps seasonally and the
passes are crossable only during the weeks it retreats. Crossing east requires knowing
the sweep, which requires the Remain, which is why Beat 6 is load-bearing on the map as
well as the plot.

### 6. The Glassing — *Act III*

The high plateau. Vitrified patches where centuries of concentrated light fused the
silica; walking it means walking on frozen glare. Empty — no Bole has stood here since
the dispersal.

It is unmistakably, measurably **too small**. The entire capacity problem is visible in
one establishing shot: this is the last lit ground, there is room for perhaps four
Boles, and there are eleven.

### 7. The Senreach — *Act II tentpole / Act III return*

Past the Glassing, the standing dead: forty-one root-crowns in a rough ring, fused at
the base, canopies collapsed millennia ago. **Hollow Sen** is the largest and the only
one intact enough to core.

The Senreach reads as a graveyard but is architecturally a **diagram** — from the
Glassing rim you can look down and see the fusion pattern from above. An attentive
player understands what happened here before the party says it aloud.

## The eleven-Bole geometry

Every Bole's Trace, followed east far enough, terminates at the Senreach. Eleven Traces,
one origin ring. Any competent auditor could have discovered this at any point in eleven
generations by walking east for two months.

Nobody did, because the Senreach lies across the Long Shade, and the Long Shade crossing
requires Remain knowledge, and Bole society does not ask the Remain for things.

**The cover-up survived on snobbery, not conspiracy.** The map is complicit.

## Traversal

- **Trace-dating as navigation.** Instead of a quest marker, the party is given a Trace
  and a target depth. Follow it, sample it, find where the stratigraphy jumps — that is
  where a Bole did something it did not record. Fully diegetic, and it makes the Coring
  the party's navigator.
- **Boles are vertical towns.** Crown / Mid / Root are three districts with separate
  economies, populations, and access rules. The player spends more time at Root, and it
  shows.
- **Boles move between acts, barely.** A player who carefully notes a Bole's position in
  Act I will find it shifted a visible but nearly useless distance by Act III. The point
  is to make the normal pace *feel* hopeless.

## The Act III transformation

When the Long Dusk lands early, Boles begin to **drive**. The map visibly changes in the
final act, for the first and only time. Three consequences to build around:

1. **Fresh Traces.** A driving Bole carves new furrow through whatever lies in front of
   it. Roads used all game are destroyed by the cities the player has been helping.
2. **The Underloam is in the way.** The most direct line from the Braid to the Glassing
   runs through Remain settlement. Bole society's emergency survival physically
   bulldozes the people who refused to join it, eleven generations after refusing to
   save them. Nobody has to be evil for this; it is simply the shortest route.
3. **Arrival order becomes the selection.** Nobody votes on who gets the Glassing. The
   Boles that drive hardest arrive first and hold ground, and the Boles that drive
   hardest are the ones that can afford to wreck their own Striders doing it. Anneal can.
   Cordwain cannot.

Act III's real horror is watching the selection happen a second time by market outcome
instead of by committee — and realizing the first one may have been the more humane
version.
