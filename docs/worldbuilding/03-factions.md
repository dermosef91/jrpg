# Factions

No villains. Five incompatible survival ethics, each internally coherent, each with
adherents the player will like.

## The Pruners

**Position:** Some failing Boles should be deliberately released — allowed to die, or
actively cut loose — so their sunwood reserves and canopy allocation can preserve
Boles with better survival odds.

Utilitarian, actuarial, and correct on the arithmetic. Pruners are not sadists; they
are the people who did the math first and are unwilling to pretend they didn't.
Dominant on Anneal's council.

**Failure mode:** the math is only as good as the survival projections, and the
projections are produced by the same people who benefit from them.

## Stillwood Faith

**Position:** Migration is a betrayal of the Striders. A Strider driven to chase light
is an animal whipped uphill. Better to stop, stay, and let the organism die in place
with dignity — and die with it.

Not a death cult and not irrational. Stillwood communities are well-run, well-provisioned,
and publicly scheduled: everyone knows the year their light fails, and they plan for it
the way other cultures plan for a harvest festival.

**Failure mode:** the doctrine binds children who did not choose it.

## The Splicers

**Position:** The species was engineered in the first place. Engineer it further —
faster migration, broader light tolerance, forked root-crowns — and stop treating an
agricultural product as sacred.

Aggressive, experimental, and the only faction generating genuinely new capability.
Concentrated on Marrow.

**Failure mode:** they ship untested grafts under deadline pressure and the organism
pays. See Act II.

## The Undercanopy Compact

**Position:** Sunwood rationing formulae were set under different Ember conditions and
must be renegotiated. Root districts bear the medical cost of a distribution model
nobody has revisited in three cohorts.

Explicitly a labor movement working through Ring Council process — organized political
pressure, not an underground. They file. They petition. They strike.

**Failure mode:** process takes years, and they may not have years.

## The Remain

**Position:** Bole society is a wasteful, hierarchical, historically amnesiac
arrangement that the Remain declined to join and have never regretted declining.

They are not monsters, obstacles, or noble primitives. They are a settled, competent,
materially poorer society with functioning infrastructure and an unbroken oral history.
Their region should play as a **town, not a dungeon** — the design goal is that the
party arrives expecting wilderness and finds a civic center.

**Failure mode:** none available to them. They are the only faction whose position has
already been empirically validated, and they know it, and it has not made them generous.

---

## Cross-cutting note

Each playable party member comes from a Bole that embodies one of these positions
(`06-cast.md`). Party arguments are faction arguments. They are never resolved by
consensus, and the game should not stage a scene where they are.
