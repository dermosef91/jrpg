# The World

## The Ember

The sun is called the Ember. It is not cursed, sealed, stolen, or dying by anyone's
design — it has entered a natural variable phase: pulses of intense output followed
by unpredictable dim stretches, some lasting a season, some lasting years. A long dim
stretch is a **Long Dusk**.

Nobody can affect this. There is no ritual, weapon, or sacrifice that changes the
Ember's behavior, and every character who has looked into it knows that. Civilization's
entire relationship with its star is *forecasting and adaptation*.

Two properties matter mechanically and geographically:

1. **Output** is dropping.
2. **Angle** is dropping. As the Ember sits lower, only elevated ground clears the
   horizon for a useful fraction of the day.

Property 2 is why the endgame migration goes uphill and east. It is derivable by
anyone with a stick and one season of shadow measurements, which is why the coming
crisis is universally understood rather than revealed.

## Striders

**Striders** are city-scale migratory megaflora — selectively bred and grafted by
early inhabitants over centuries — whose root-crowns creep across the land chasing
light. There is no single world-tree. There are dozens of Striders, a slow-motion herd,
each with its own migration history and its own growth personality: some stubborn,
some sickly, some split into twin trunks after old damage.

Normal migration speed is **meters to tens of meters per year**. Generational, not
observable. Within one human lifetime a Strider moves the length of a town.

Striders can be **driven** — forced into a growth surge by aggressive Grafting, moving
hundreds of meters to a couple of kilometers in a season, at severe and permanent cost
to the organism's health. Driving is an emergency measure and a scandal. See
`05-map.md` for what happens when everyone does it at once.

## Boles

A **Bole** is a city built into and around a living Strider's canopy and root-crown.
Eleven Boles exist. They are vertical settlements with three districts:

- **Crown** — direct light, highest status, greatest structural risk. The canopy sways;
  storms hit hardest at the top. Crown residents rarely descend.
- **Mid** — the working city. Most trade, most craft, most people.
- **Root** — sheltered, stable, permanently shaded. Dependent on rationed light.

This is a genuine trade-off rather than a simple hierarchy. Crown is not merely
"the rich part"; it is the part that loses people to weather.

## Sunwood

**Sunwood** is resin that crystallizes stored light. It functions simultaneously as
currency, fuel, and medicine — a Root-district child raised without sunwood
supplementation does not develop correctly. This makes the economy and the public
health system the same system, and makes every rationing decision a medical one.

## Ring Councils and cohort politics

Political power sits in **Ring Councils**. Seats are allotted by **migration-era
cohort** — which era of Ember conditions you were born into — rather than by wealth or
district.

The consequence: the primary political fault line is **generational**, not economic.
Elders who remember fertile cycles and want to preserve the customs that worked then,
versus younger cohorts who have known nothing but scarcity and want structural change.
Crown and Root both contain both factions, which keeps the politics from collapsing
into class melodrama.

Citizens are formally named `[Given] [Bole]-of-the-[cohort ordinal]` — e.g. **Verity
Anneal-of-the-Ninth**. A name states where you are from and which world you remember.

## The Remain

Bole society calls them **Groundwalkers** and regards them as people who never
developed tree-cities. They call themselves **the Remain**, and the name is not
poetic — see `04-story.md`.

They live at ground level in ordinary old-growth forest, on diffuse light, in
permanent settlement. Poorer than the Boles, smaller in number, and entirely intact.
They keep no rings, so their history is oral, unedited, and eleven generations longer
than anyone's in the Boles.
